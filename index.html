Why we made it

Most of us were never taught how our own mind works

Mental health problems are common, the myths around them keep people quiet, and the good news about treatment is the least known part. A leaflet gets read once. A game gets played again and again, so we used one.

How to play

Survive the waves, calm the stress, beat the boss

  1. Make your characterPick skin, hair, outfit and an accessory, then step into your room.
  2. Survive three wavesEnemies are heavy thoughts. Waves are randomized every run, so no two runs match.
  3. Pick a habitBetween waves choose an upgrade like The Pause, Boundary or A Friend. Each one carries a short tip and its source.
  4. Beat the bossThree phases, three health bars, and a conversation in between. Then travel to the next room.
  5. Read the LetterEach boss leaves a Letter in your Library: what it is, myths and facts, what helps, and when to reach out.

The science

Every fact in the game has a source

All health statements live in one register, each tied to a WHO, NHS, NIMH or MedlinePlus page and labelled as a definition, a fact or guidance. An independent reviewer checked every sentence against its page, and we rewrote the lines that went further than the source.

A letter left behind by the Loop

Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

  • MythOCD just means liking things tidy. Not all habits are compulsions.1
  • MythDoing the ritual fixes the worry. Relief is only temporary; the obsession and anxiety soon return.2
  • FactHaving OCD is not your fault.2
  • FactTalking therapy can help with OCD.1,2

1. NIMH, Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder. 2. NHS, Obsessive compulsive disorder (OCD). For awareness only. Not a diagnosis or medical advice.

Mechanics that mirror the scienceThe NHS describes a cycle: a compulsion relieves anxiety for a moment, then the worry returns. In the Perfect Room, stepping on an urge tile lowers stress briefly, then it comes back and the boss heals. Letting the urge fade weakens the boss.
Careful about limitsThe game never diagnoses. It says OCD is unlikely to get better without proper treatment and support, and every Letter tells players when to talk to a doctor.
